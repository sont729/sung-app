About This Translation

태초에 · Genesis and Exodus in Korean and English, with Hebrew

The Genesis and Exodus texts in 태초에 consist of Korean and English translations produced for this app using the Hebrew text as the source. Both languages contain all 50 chapters and 1,533 verses of Genesis and all 40 chapters and 1,213 verses of Exodus (90 chapters and 2,746 verses in total); choose the language in Settings. The source is the Westminster Leningrad Codex (WLC), based on the Leningrad Codex. AI tools were used to draft, revise, and review the translations, commentary, and narratives, and to create the images. “Our own translation” means a translation produced for this app; it does not mean that AI was absent or that the text is free of errors.

There is a record of modern copyrighted Korean Bible translations being opened during review on September 3, 2026. The scope of that access and its effect on the current text remain unverified. We do not claim that existing translations were never consulted. Legal review of the actual production, access, and revision records, human review, and rights approval remain outstanding.

This is not an authorized translation for worship or education. It was made to help readers see what the Hebrew text says and how it says it, alongside the Bible they use in church.

Commentary explains translation choices

Genesis and Exodus are both free in both languages. Where a verse has commentary, its button explains the choices at that point and why a reading was selected. Some commentary covers a whole chapter. This does not mean that every verse has commentary. We also acknowledge places where Korean or English cannot reproduce Hebrew wordplay.

Other readings are acknowledged

Some passages are read differently across denominations: the “sons of God” in Genesis 6:2, the Nephilim, the extent of the flood, the target of Noah's curse, and the two nations in 25:23, for example.

We aim to make room for interpretation visible. Commentary distinguishes wording selected for readability from other possible readings. Words supplied from context are not described as if each were separately present in the Hebrew.

Why New Testament passages are not used to explain Genesis

Several Genesis passages have long been read as pointing to Christ: the woman's offspring in 3:15, Melchizedek in chapter 14, Isaac in chapter 22, the stairway in 28:12, and Shiloh in 49:10.

We do not explain those passages by adding New Testament verses, for two reasons.

First, it is outside the task this app has undertaken. The app shows what the Hebrew text says in its immediate setting. Reading it back through the New Testament belongs to preaching, commentary, and Bible study, whose practitioners are better placed to do that work.

Second, doing so can narrow several readings to one. For example, the commentary explains several possibilities for שילה (Shiloh) in 49:10. The translation retains “Shiloh,” and the narrative does not establish it as the appearance of a particular person. Where more than one reading is possible, we aim not to present one interpretation as the only fact.

The app does not reject how the New Testament reads such passages. That question falls outside the scope it has taken on.

The narratives are not scripture

Each language's 413 scene narratives (222 in Genesis and 191 in Exodus) were produced and revised for this app with AI tools. They are not biblical text, are always visually distinguished from it, and can be hidden entirely through the view modes.

The narratives are separate creative reconstructions. They may include sensory details, actions, and psychological descriptions not explicitly stated in the Hebrew. Those descriptions are not the biblical text or established historical fact. Direct quotations are checked for character-for-character agreement with this app's translation of the relevant passage.

Please report mistakes

AI was used in production and review. Automated checks and internal review cannot fully guarantee accuracy, theology, or literary quality, and do not replace human theological review or legal review. Please identify the chapter and verse when reporting a problem. We will check it against the source again, make corrections, and describe changes in updates.
